Crochet Abbreviations: US & UK Terms Chart

Crochet patterns are written in a kind of shorthand. This chart lists the abbreviations you'll meet most often, with the US and UK names side by side and a short note on what each one means.

Watch out: the US and UK systems use the same words for different stitches. A UK “double crochet (dc)” is a US “single crochet (sc)”. Always check which terms your pattern uses. Our US vs UK terms guide explains how to tell.

Tips for using abbreviations

  • Read the pattern's own key first. Designers sometimes use their own abbreviations, and the key at the top of the pattern always wins.
  • Check the stitch counts. If your count matches the number at the end of the round, you've read the abbreviations correctly.
  • Mark the first stitch of each round with a stitch marker so you know where the round ends.
  • Track your rounds. In Crochet Tracker you can tap chips like sc, inc and BLO to type patterns quickly, then tick each round off as you go.

Abbreviation FAQ

What does “sc” mean in crochet?

“sc” is the US abbreviation for single crochet, the short basic stitch. In UK terms the same stitch is called double crochet (dc).

What does “inc” mean in a crochet pattern?

“inc” means increase: work two stitches into the same stitch. Each increase adds one stitch to your count.

What does “mr” stand for?

“mr” stands for magic ring, also called a magic circle or magic loop. It's an adjustable loop that lets you start a round with no hole in the middle.

What is the difference between BLO and FLO?

BLO means back loop only and FLO means front loop only. Instead of going under both loops at the top of a stitch, you go under just one, which changes the texture and shape of your work.
