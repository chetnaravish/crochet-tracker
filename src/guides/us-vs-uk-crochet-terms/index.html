US vs UK Crochet Terms

US and UK patterns use the same stitches but give them different names. The tricky part: some names exist in both systems and mean different stitches. This page shows them side by side so you can see the difference at a glance.

The one rule to remember

USsc same
stitch UKdc

The UK name is always one step taller than the US name. US single crochet is UK double crochet, US double crochet is UK treble, and so on. Your hands do exactly the same thing; only the word on the pattern changes.

The trap: “dc” means two different stitches

Read this pattern line: R2: 2 dc in each st around (24). What you make depends on where the pattern comes from:

USdc In a US pattern

Tall stitch. Yarn over first, then pull through 2 loops twice. Makes open, airy fabric.

UKdc In a UK pattern

Short stitch (the one US patterns call sc). No yarn over first, pull through 2 loops once. Makes tight fabric.

So: if you make US dc for a UK pattern, your piece comes out about twice as tall and full of holes. Amigurumi stuffing would show through. Always check which terms a pattern uses before you start.

How to tell which terms a pattern uses

  • 1Read the top of the patternGood patterns say “US terms” or “UK terms” near the materials list.
  • 2Look for “sc”Single crochet only exists in US terms. See sc? It is a US pattern.
  • 3Look for “htr” or “ss”These two are only used in UK patterns.
  • 4Amigurumi full of “dc”?Amigurumi uses the shortest stitch, so a toy made mostly in “dc” is almost surely UK.

Why are there two systems?

Both systems count something, but not the same thing:

  • US names count how many times you “yarn over and pull through two loops”. Once = single crochet, twice = double crochet.
  • UK names count the loops on your hook after the first pull-up. Two loops = double crochet, three loops = treble.

Both make sense on their own, but the count is off by one, so every name shifts by one step. Patterns from the US, Canada and most online designers use US terms. Patterns from the UK, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand and South Africa often use UK terms.

US vs UK FAQ

Is UK double crochet the same as US single crochet?

Yes. UK double crochet (dc) and US single crochet (sc) are exactly the same stitch, with two different names.

Do Australian patterns use US or UK terms?

Most Australian and New Zealand patterns use UK terms, but always check the pattern notes, since many designers now write in US terms for an international audience.

Which terms do the free patterns on this site use?

All free patterns here use US terms, and say so at the top. The stitch words in each pattern link to a short explanation with the UK name.

Are the hook sizes different too?

Hook sizes are usually given in millimetres everywhere, which is the safest number to use. Older US patterns may give letter sizes (like G or H), and older UK patterns may use number sizes, so check a conversion table if there's no millimetre size.
