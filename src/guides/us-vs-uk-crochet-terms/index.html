US vs UK Crochet Terms

Crochet has two naming systems, and they disagree. A “double crochet” in a UK pattern is a different, shorter stitch than a “double crochet” in a US pattern. Here's how the two systems line up, and how to tell which one you're reading.

Why are there two systems?

Both systems name stitches by counting something, but they count different things. The US system names a stitch by how many times you yarn over and pull through two loops: one for single crochet, two for double crochet. The UK system names it by how many loops are on the hook after you pull up the first loop: two loops gives a double crochet, three gives a treble.

Both make sense on their own terms. The trouble is that they shift every stitch name by one step. Patterns from the US, Canada and many online designers usually use US terms. Patterns from the UK, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand and South Africa often use UK terms.

How to tell which terms a pattern uses

  1. Look for a note at the top. Good patterns say “written in US terms” or “UK terminology”.
  2. Search for “sc”. Single crochet doesn't exist in UK terms. If you see sc, it's a US pattern.
  3. Search for “htr” or “ss”. These are UK-only abbreviations.
  4. Check the shortest stitch. If an amigurumi pattern is mostly “dc”, it's almost certainly UK, because amigurumi uses the shortest common stitch.
  5. Check the designer's location or the shop where the pattern is sold.

Quick test: make a small swatch of the first few rows. If the fabric looks far too loose and lacy for what the photo shows, you're probably using US stitches on a UK pattern.

Converting a pattern

To convert UK to US, move every stitch one step shorter in name: UK dc becomes US sc, UK tr becomes US dc, and so on. To convert US to UK, move one step taller in name. The actual stitches you make don't change at all; only the names do.

The easiest method is to write the converted version once and keep it. In Crochet Tracker you can paste the pattern, edit the rounds into the terms you prefer, and save it, so you never have to convert in your head while you crochet.

US vs UK FAQ

Is UK double crochet the same as US single crochet?

Yes. UK double crochet (dc) and US single crochet (sc) are exactly the same stitch, with two different names.

Do Australian patterns use US or UK terms?

Most Australian and New Zealand patterns use UK terms, but always check the pattern notes, since many designers now write in US terms for an international audience.

Are the hook sizes different too?

Hook sizes are usually given in millimetres everywhere, which is the safest number to use. Older US patterns may give letter sizes (like G or H), and older UK patterns may use number sizes, so check a conversion table if there's no millimetre size.
