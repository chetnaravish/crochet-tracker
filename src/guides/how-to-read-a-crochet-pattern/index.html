How to Read a Crochet Pattern

A crochet pattern can look like a secret code: “R3: (sc, inc) x6 (18)”. The good news is that the code is small and very consistent. Once you know a handful of rules, you can read almost any pattern.

1. The parts of a pattern

Most patterns follow the same order. Before you pick up your hook, read the top section, because it tells you how to interpret everything below it.

  • Materials: yarn weight and amount, hook size, plus extras like stuffing, safety eyes or a stitch marker.
  • Gauge (tension): how many stitches and rows fit in a set size. It matters for clothes; for toys it matters less.
  • Terminology: whether the pattern uses US or UK terms. This is important, because the same abbreviation can mean a different stitch.
  • Abbreviations and special stitches: the pattern's own key. If it defines something differently from a general chart, the pattern wins.
  • Notes: for example “worked in a continuous spiral, do not join rounds” or “ch 3 counts as a dc”.
  • Instructions: the rounds or rows themselves, usually one per line.
  • Finishing: how to fasten off, sew pieces together and weave in ends.

2. Rounds vs rows

Lines usually begin with a label: R1, Rnd 1 or Round 1 for work in the round, and Row 1 for flat work that you turn at the end of each row.

In the round, you keep going in the same direction. Some patterns join each round with a slip stitch; others, especially amigurumi, work in a continuous spiral with no joins. Put a stitch marker in the first stitch of every round so you can see where it ends.

A label with a range, like R3-5 or Rnds 6–10, means “do these same instructions for each of those rounds”. R3-5 is three rounds: 3, 4 and 5.

3. Reading one line, piece by piece

Read a line from left to right, one instruction at a time, separated by commas. Numbers in front of an abbreviation tell you how many stitches to make, each in its own stitch:

  • sc: one single crochet in the next stitch.
  • 2 sc or sc 2: one single crochet in each of the next two stitches.
  • 2 sc in next st or inc: two stitches in the same stitch (an increase).
  • dec or sc2tog: work two stitches together into one (a decrease).
  • sc in each st around: one single crochet in every stitch until the end of the round.
  • 6 sc in mr: six single crochet into a magic ring.

If an abbreviation is new to you, look it up in our crochet abbreviations chart.

4. Brackets, asterisks and repeats

Patterns save space by grouping instructions and saying how many times to repeat them.

  • Round brackets ( ): (sc, inc) x6 means work “1 sc, then 1 increase”, and do that whole group six times.
  • Square brackets [ ]: used for a group inside a group, e.g. [sc, (sc, inc) x2] x3, or for a stitch count at the end.
  • Asterisks *: *2 sc, inc; rep from * around means repeat the part after the asterisk until the end of the round.
  • Into the same stitch: (sc, hdc, dc) in next st means all three stitches go into one stitch. The word “in” is the clue.

Rule of thumb: if a bracket is followed by “x6” or “6 times”, it's a repeat. If it's followed by “in next st” or “in same st”, all of it goes into one stitch.

5. Stitch counts

The number at the end of a line, such as (18), [18] or – 18 sts, is the total number of stitches you should have when the round is finished. It's your built-in check.

Count your stitches at the end of each round, at least while you're learning. If the count is wrong, it's much easier to fix it now than five rounds later.

6. A worked example

Here is the start of a simple amigurumi ball, and what each line means:

LineWhat you do
R1: 6 sc in mr (6)Make a magic ring, work 6 single crochet into it and pull it closed. You have 6 stitches.
R2: inc x6 (12)Work 2 sc into every stitch. 6 stitches become 12.
R3: (sc, inc) x6 (18)1 sc in the next stitch, then 2 sc in the following one. Repeat 6 times for 18.
R4: (2 sc, inc) x6 (24)1 sc in each of the next 2 stitches, then an increase. Repeat 6 times for 24.
R5-9: sc around (24)Five rounds of 1 sc in every stitch. The count stays at 24 and the piece starts to curve.
R10: (2 sc, dec) x6 (18)2 sc, then a decrease. Repeat 6 times. The ball starts to close.

7. Tips for not losing your place

  • Use a stitch marker in the first stitch of each round, and move it up every round.
  • Tick off each round as soon as you finish it. In Crochet Tracker you tap Done and the next round is shown big and ready.
  • Read the whole line before starting it, so you notice “in same st” or a repeat that ends early.
  • Count, then continue. Match your count to the number at the end of the line.
  • Note your changes. If you change a hook or colour, write it in the pattern notes so you can repeat it next time.
