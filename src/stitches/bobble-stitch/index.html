How to Crochet: Bobble Stitch

A bobble is a round bump made of unfinished double crochets. You start each one, stop halfway, then close them all together.

Bobbles pop out on the side facing away from you, so in rows they are usually worked on the wrong side. Check your pattern for how many stitches each bobble uses. Five is common.

Step by step

  1. Make an unfinished dc. Yarn over, insert into the stitch, yarn over and pull up a loop, yarn over and pull through 2 loops. Stop here. ✓ 2 loops on your hook.
  2. Repeat in the same stitch. Make another unfinished dc in the same stitch, 4 more times. Each one adds one loop. ✓ 6 loops on your hook.
  3. Close the bobble. Yarn over and pull through all 6 loops. ✓ 1 loop left.
  4. Lock and push. Many patterns add a ch 1 here. Then push the bobble to the other side of your work with your finger. ✓ A round bump stands out on the other side.
  5. Next stitch. Make a short stitch next, like a single crochet. It keeps the bobble standing out. ✓ The bobble does not flatten.

In a chart

Crochet charts draw each stitch as a small symbol. Here is the bobble stitch on top of a row of single crochet. Symbols can look a little different from one designer to the next, so check the key of your chart.

Where you'll use it

  • Textured blankets and pillows
  • Bobble hats and mittens
  • Bumpy “berries” and grapes in amigurumi
  • Decorative borders

Common mistakes

  • Finishing each dc fully. Only pull through two loops each time. If you finish the dc, you get a shell instead of a bobble.
  • Bobbles on the wrong side. They pop away from you. If they show on the back, work the bobble row on the other side.
  • Miscounting loops. For a 5-dc bobble you should have 6 loops before closing. Count before the last yarn over.

Tip: Hold the last stitch before the bobble a little tight. It pinches the base so the bobble pops more.
