How to Crochet: Chain Stitch (ch)

The chain is the most basic crochet stitch: you pull a new loop of yarn through the loop already on your hook. A row of chains, called a foundation chain, is the starting point for most flat projects like scarves and blankets.

Chains are also used inside patterns to add height at the start of a row (a turning chain), to make spaces and lacy holes, and to create loops for buttons or hanging.

Step by step

  1. Make a slip knot and put it on your hook. Tighten it so it moves freely but isn't loose.
  2. Hold the hook in your dominant hand and the yarn in the other, with the working yarn over your index finger for tension.
  3. Wrap the yarn over the hook from back to front. This is a “yarn over”.
  4. Turn the hook slightly so its throat faces down and pull the yarn through the loop on your hook. You have made one chain.
  5. Repeat the yarn over and pull through for each chain your pattern asks for.
  6. Move your holding fingers up every few chains so you are always pinching close to the hook. This keeps the chains even.

US vs UK name

In US patterns this is called Chain (ch). In UK patterns it's Chain (ch). The name is the same in both systems, which makes this one easy.

Where you'll use it

  • Foundation chains for rows
  • Turning chains at the start of rows
  • Chain spaces in lace and granny squares
  • Hanging loops and ties

Common mistakes

  • Chaining too tightly. A tight foundation chain makes the first row hard to work into and pulls the edge in. Try a hook one size bigger for the chain.
  • Counting the loop on the hook. The loop on your hook never counts as a chain; neither does the slip knot.
  • Twisting the chain. When joining a chain into a ring, check it isn't twisted first.

Tip: To count chains, look at the front: each chain is a small “V”. Count the Vs, starting from the one just below your hook.
