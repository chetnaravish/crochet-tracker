How to Crochet: Double Crochet (dc)

Double crochet is a tall stitch that builds fabric quickly and drapes nicely. It's the main stitch in granny squares, many blankets and shawls, and lacy patterns.

Because each stitch is about three chains tall, rows grow fast, which makes double crochet satisfying for big projects.

Abbreviation
dc
US name
Double crochet (dc)
UK name
Treble (tr)
Height
3 chains tall
Level
Beginner

Step by step

  1. Yarn over before inserting your hook.
  2. Insert your hook into the stitch under both top loops.
  3. Yarn over and pull up a loop. You have three loops on your hook.
  4. Yarn over and pull through the first two loops. Two loops remain.
  5. Yarn over and pull through the last two loops. One double crochet is complete.
  6. At the start of a row, chain 3 to reach the height of the stitches. In most patterns the ch 3 counts as the first dc.

US vs UK name

In US patterns this is called Double crochet (dc). In UK patterns it's Treble (tr). The stitch is identical; only the name changes. Not sure which system your pattern uses? See our US vs UK crochet terms guide.

Where you'll use it

  • Granny squares
  • Blankets and throws
  • Shawls and lace
  • Round coasters and mandalas

Common mistakes

  • Gaps at the edges. If the ch 3 looks loose, try ch 2 instead, or start with a “stacked single crochet”.
  • Changing the stitch count by accident. When the ch 3 counts as a stitch, skip the first stitch and work the last dc into the top of the turning chain.
  • Pulling through all three loops at once. That makes a different stitch. Double crochet is always “two, then two”.

Tip: Say “yarn over, in, pull up – over, through two – over, through two” in your head. That rhythm keeps every stitch the same height.
