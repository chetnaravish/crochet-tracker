How to Crochet: Single Crochet (sc)

Single crochet is the workhorse of crochet. It makes a firm, dense fabric with small gaps, which is exactly what you want for amigurumi (so stuffing doesn't show), bags, coasters and sturdy edges.

It's also the stitch most other stitches are built on, so once you can single crochet evenly, the taller stitches feel familiar.

Step by step

  1. Insert the hook. Look at the top of the next stitch: you will see a sideways “V”. Push the hook under both strands of the V, from the front of your work to the back. ✓ The loop from before is still on your hook.
  2. Yarn over. Keep the hook in the stitch. Wrap the working yarn (the yarn coming from the ball) over the hook from back to front, so it lands in the little hook at the tip. ✓ The yarn is caught in the hook.
  3. Pull up a loop. Turn the hook slightly downwards and pull it back out through the stitch, bringing the yarn with it. ✓ 2 loops on your hook.
  4. Yarn over again. Wrap the yarn over the hook once more, the same way as before. ✓ The new wrap sits in front of the 2 loops.
  5. Pull through both loops. Pull the yarn through both loops on the hook in one smooth movement. ✓ 1 loop left. One single crochet is done.
  6. Carry on. Move to the next V and repeat steps 1 to 5. At the end of a row, chain 1 and turn your work. This chain 1 does not count as a stitch. ✓ Count your stitches at the end of every row or round.

US vs UK name

In US patterns this is called Single crochet (sc). In UK patterns it's Double crochet (dc). The stitch is identical; only the name changes. Not sure which system your pattern uses? See our US vs UK crochet terms guide.

Where you'll use it

  • Amigurumi
  • Coasters, baskets and bags
  • Borders and edges
  • Dense, warm fabric

Common mistakes

  • Missing the last stitch of a row. The final stitch can hide behind the turning chain. Count your stitches every few rows.
  • Working into the turning chain. In single crochet the ch 1 doesn't count, so skip it and start in the first real stitch.
  • Inconsistent tension. Uneven loops make bumpy fabric. Let the yarn flow through your fingers at the same pace.
  • Confusing US and UK terms. If a UK pattern says “dc”, it means this stitch.

Tip: For amigurumi, many crocheters use the “yarn under” variation (yarn over from front to back on the first step) to make tight little X-shaped stitches.
