How to Crochet: Magic Ring

The magic ring is an adjustable loop of yarn that you crochet your first round into. When you pull the tail, the loop closes up tight, so the centre of your work has no hole. That is why almost every amigurumi pattern starts with “6 sc in mr”.

It feels fiddly the first few times because you are holding a loose loop instead of a solid chain. After a little practice it becomes the quickest and neatest way to start anything worked in the round: balls, toys, hats, coasters and granny-style circles.

Step by step

  1. Lay the yarn tail over your fingers and wrap the working yarn around them to form a loop where the yarn crosses itself.
  2. Insert your hook through the loop from front to back, catch the working yarn and pull it back through the loop.
  3. Yarn over and pull through the loop on your hook to make one chain. This chain locks the ring and does not count as a stitch.
  4. Insert the hook into the ring again, so you are working over both the ring and the tail, and make your first stitch (usually a single crochet).
  5. Keep working stitches into the ring until you have the number your pattern asks for, for example 6 sc.
  6. Hold the stitches and pull the yarn tail firmly. The ring shrinks and closes the centre.
  7. Join with a slip stitch to the first stitch, or simply carry on into round 2 if you are working in a continuous spiral.

US vs UK name

In US patterns this is called Magic ring (mr). In UK patterns it's Magic loop / magic circle. The stitch is identical; only the name changes. Not sure which system your pattern uses? See our US vs UK crochet terms guide.

Where you'll use it

  • Amigurumi heads, bodies and balls
  • Round coasters and mandalas
  • Top-down hats
  • Flowers and small motifs

Common mistakes

  • Pulling the tail before all stitches are in. Once the ring is closed you can't add more stitches, so count first, then pull.
  • Working only into the ring and missing the tail. Work over both strands so the tail is caught and the ring stays secure.
  • Counting the locking chain as a stitch. It only secures the loop; don't include it in your round 1 count.
  • Not weaving the tail in well. Weave it back and forth through the first round so the ring can't loosen with use.

Tip: If the magic ring keeps slipping, start with a chain of 2 and work your round 1 stitches into the second chain from the hook. It leaves a tiny hole, but it's a great stepping stone.
