How to Crochet: Half Double Crochet (hdc)

Half double crochet sits between single and double crochet in height. It grows faster than single crochet but still makes a fairly solid fabric, which is why it's a favourite for hats, blankets and scarves.

Its special feature is the third loop: a horizontal bar on the back of each stitch that you can work into for a knit-like ribbed look.

Abbreviation
hdc
US name
Half double crochet (hdc)
UK name
Half treble (htr)
Height
2 chains tall
Level
Beginner

Step by step

  1. Yarn over before inserting your hook.
  2. Insert your hook into the stitch under both top loops.
  3. Yarn over and pull up a loop. You now have three loops on your hook.
  4. Yarn over once more.
  5. Pull through all three loops at once. One half double crochet is complete.
  6. In rows, chain 2 to turn. Patterns differ on whether the ch 2 counts as a stitch, so check yours.

US vs UK name

In US patterns this is called Half double crochet (hdc). In UK patterns it's Half treble (htr). The stitch is identical; only the name changes. Not sure which system your pattern uses? See our US vs UK crochet terms guide.

Where you'll use it

  • Hats and beanies
  • Blankets and scarves
  • Ribbing using the third loop
  • Quick, warm fabric

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the first yarn over. Without it you've made a single crochet.
  • Pulling the loops too tight. Drawing through three loops is easier if you pull the first loop up to the height of a chain-2.
  • Losing a stitch at the edges. Decide whether your turning chain counts and stay consistent.

Tip: Working hdc into the third loop only creates a raised line that looks like knitted ribbing. It's a great trick for hat brims.
