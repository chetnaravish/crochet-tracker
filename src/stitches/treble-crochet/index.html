How to Crochet: Treble Crochet (tr)

Treble crochet is a tall, airy stitch. It starts with two yarn overs, and then you work off the loops two at a time, three times.

You will see it in lacy shawls, tall petals and fast blankets. Once you know double crochet, treble is just one extra yarn over and one extra “pull through two”.

Abbreviation
tr
US name
Treble Crochet (tr)
UK name
Double treble (dtr)
Height
4 chains tall
Chart symbol
Level
Beginner+

Step by step

  1. Yarn over twice. Before you insert the hook, wrap the yarn over the hook two times. ✓ 3 loops on your hook.
  2. Insert the hook. Push the hook under both strands of the “V” on top of the next stitch, from front to back. ✓ Both wraps are still on the hook.
  3. Pull up a loop. Yarn over and pull the yarn back through the stitch. ✓ 4 loops on your hook.
  4. Pull through two. Yarn over and pull through the first 2 loops only. ✓ 3 loops on your hook.
  5. Pull through two again. Yarn over and pull through the next 2 loops. ✓ 2 loops on your hook.
  6. Pull through the last two. Yarn over and pull through the last 2 loops. ✓ 1 loop left. One treble crochet is done.
  7. Turning chain. At the start of a row, chain 4. In most patterns this ch 4 counts as the first tr. ✓ Easy way to remember: after pulling up the loop, it is “pull through two” three times.

In a chart

Crochet charts draw each stitch as a small symbol. Here is the treble crochet on top of a row of single crochet. Symbols can look a little different from one designer to the next, so check the key of your chart.

Where you'll use it

  • Flower petals, like the petals of the mini sunflower
  • Lacy shawls and wraps
  • Tall, quick blanket rows
  • Granny-style motifs

Common mistakes

  • Only one yarn over at the start. That makes a double crochet. Treble always starts with two wraps on the hook.
  • Leaning or wobbly stitches. Pull up the first loop to the height of the row, not higher, and keep the same tension for every “pull through two”.
  • Mixing up the names. In UK patterns “tr” means the US double crochet. Check whether your pattern uses US or UK terms.

Tip: Count the pull-throughs out loud: “two, two, two”. Three times means treble.
