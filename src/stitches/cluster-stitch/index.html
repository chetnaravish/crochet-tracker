How to Crochet: Cluster Stitch (cl)

A cluster joins several stitches at the top. You start a double crochet in each of the next stitches, stop halfway, and close them all together.

Here we make a 3-dc cluster, also written dc3tog. Patterns can ask for other sizes, so always read what the pattern means by “cl”.

Step by step

  1. Unfinished dc in the first stitch. Yarn over, insert into the next stitch, pull up a loop, yarn over and pull through 2 loops. Stop here. ✓ 2 loops on your hook.
  2. Unfinished dc in the next stitch. Do the same in the next stitch. ✓ 3 loops on your hook.
  3. And the next. Do the same in the stitch after that. ✓ 4 loops on your hook.
  4. Join them. Yarn over and pull through all 4 loops. ✓ 1 loop left. One cluster is done.
  5. Good to know. A 3-stitch cluster uses three stitches and makes one, so it also works as a decrease. ✓ Your stitch count goes down by 2 for each cluster.

In a chart

Crochet charts draw each stitch as a small symbol. Here is the cluster stitch on top of a row of single crochet. Symbols can look a little different from one designer to the next, so check the key of your chart.

Where you'll use it

  • Lacy shawls and the “cluster stitch” blanket
  • Shaping, as a tall decrease
  • Petals and leaves
  • Textured dishcloths

Common mistakes

  • Working all three into one stitch. That is a bobble. A cluster spreads over three different stitches, unless the pattern says otherwise.
  • Wrong stitch count. Each cluster turns three stitches into one. Count the row afterwards so you don’t lose stitches by accident.
  • Tight top. Pull the final yarn over through slowly and with a relaxed hand, or the cluster will pucker.

Tip: Clusters look best when the stitch after them is a chain or a short stitch, which gives them room to fan out.
