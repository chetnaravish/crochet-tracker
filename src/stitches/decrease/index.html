How to Crochet: Decrease (dec)

A decrease joins two stitches into one, removing one stitch from the count. It's how crochet gets narrower, for example when closing the top of a ball or shaping a toy's head.

The standard method is single crochet two together (sc2tog). For amigurumi, many people prefer the invisible decrease, which hides almost completely.

Step by step

  1. Insert your hook into the next stitch, yarn over and pull up a loop (two loops on the hook).
  2. Insert your hook into the following stitch, yarn over and pull up a loop (three loops on the hook).
  3. Yarn over and pull through all three loops. One decrease (sc2tog) is complete.
  4. For an invisible decrease: insert your hook into the front loop only of the next stitch, then straight into the front loop only of the stitch after it (three loops on the hook).
  5. Yarn over and pull through the two front loops (two loops on the hook).
  6. Yarn over and pull through both loops on the hook to finish the invisible decrease.

US vs UK name

In US patterns this is called Decrease (dec / sc2tog). In UK patterns it's Decrease (dec / dc2tog). The stitch is identical; only the name changes. Not sure which system your pattern uses? See our US vs UK crochet terms guide.

Where you'll use it

  • Closing amigurumi pieces
  • Shaping heads, necks and limbs
  • Tapering flat pieces
  • Fitting hats and garments

Common mistakes

  • Leaving gaps. Standard decreases can leave small holes in amigurumi. Try the invisible decrease.
  • Pulling through only two loops. For sc2tog the final pull goes through all three loops.
  • Forgetting to stuff before the last decreases. Stuff the piece when the opening is still big enough, then finish decreasing.

Tip: When a pattern says “dec x6” on the last round, leave a long tail, thread it through the front loops of the remaining stitches and pull tight to close the hole neatly.
