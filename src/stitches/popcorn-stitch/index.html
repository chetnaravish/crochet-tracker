How to Crochet: Popcorn Stitch

A popcorn is a group of complete double crochets in one stitch, folded closed at the top so it pops out like a little cup.

It looks a bit like a bobble, but it is made differently. Each stitch is finished first, and then you take the hook out and close the group. That makes it firmer and more 3D.

Step by step

  1. Five dc in one stitch. Work 5 complete double crochets into the same stitch, like a small fan. ✓ A fan of 5 stitches, 1 loop on your hook.
  2. Drop the loop. Take the hook out of the loop. Keep the loop big so it does not undo. ✓ The loop is waiting, open.
  3. Go into the first dc. Insert the hook from front to back into the top of the first dc of the fan. ✓ The hook is in the first dc, the dropped loop is next to it.
  4. Pull the loop through. Catch the dropped loop and pull it through the first dc. ✓ The fan folds closed and pops towards you.
  5. Lock it. Chain 1 to lock the popcorn. Most patterns don’t count this chain as a stitch. ✓ The popcorn stays closed.

In a chart

Crochet charts draw each stitch as a small symbol. Here is the popcorn stitch on top of a row of single crochet. Symbols can look a little different from one designer to the next, so check the key of your chart.

Where you'll use it

  • Popcorn flowers and squares
  • Thick textured blankets
  • Bag panels and cushions
  • Amigurumi details like buttons or berries

Common mistakes

  • Losing the dropped loop. Pull the loop a little longer before you remove the hook, or hold it with a finger.
  • Popcorn pops to the back. Insert the hook into the first dc from front to back for it to pop toward you. Back to front pops it the other way.
  • Mixing it up with a bobble. A bobble uses unfinished stitches closed together. A popcorn uses finished stitches folded shut.

Tip: Popcorns eat yarn fast. If a pattern has many of them, buy a little extra yarn.
