How to Crochet in the Front Loop Only and Back Loop Only (FLO / BLO)

The top of every stitch looks like a little “V” made of two loops. Normally you go under both. FLO and BLO tell you to use just one of them.

Working into one loop leaves the other loop as a thin line on the fabric. That line makes ribbing, sharp edges on amigurumi bases, and a place to add stitches later.

Abbreviation
FLO / BLO
US name
Front & Back Loop Only (FLO / BLO)
UK name
Front / back loop only
Height
Same as the stitch used
Level
Beginner

Step by step

  1. Find the two loops. Look down at the top of a stitch. You will see a sideways “V”. The strand nearest to you is the front loop. The strand farther away is the back loop. ✓ You can point to both loops.
  2. Back loop only (BLO). Push the hook under the back loop only. Go in from the middle of the V and come out at the back. ✓ Only one strand is over your hook.
  3. Front loop only (FLO). Push the hook under the front loop only, from the front towards the back. ✓ The back loop stays free.
  4. Work the stitch. Now finish the stitch as normal, for example a single crochet: yarn over, pull up a loop, yarn over, pull through both loops. ✓ 1 loop on your hook.
  5. What you will see. The loop you did not use makes a line or ridge across your work. In rows, working BLO on every row makes a stretchy rib. ✓ A neat ridge appears along the round or row.

Where you'll use it

  • Flat bottoms on amigurumi baskets and toys (BLO round)
  • Ribbing for hats and cuffs
  • Leaving loops for frills or a second layer (FLO)
  • Textured blankets

Common mistakes

  • Using the wrong loop when you turn. Front and back are always judged from where you are sitting now, not from the right side of the work.
  • Grabbing the third loop. Under the V there is a third bar on hdc stitches. That is a different technique. Stay with the top two loops.
  • Tight stitches. Single loops are thinner, so it is easy to pull too hard. Keep your tension a little looser.

Tip: Put your hook at the top of a stitch and say “near loop, far loop”. Front is near, back is far, whichever side faces you.
