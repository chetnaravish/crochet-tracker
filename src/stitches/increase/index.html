How to Crochet: Increase (inc)

An increase means working two stitches into the same stitch. Each increase adds one stitch to the round or row, which is how crochet grows wider.

In amigurumi, increases are what turn a flat circle into a ball. A typical round like “(sc, inc) x6” spaces six increases evenly so the circle stays flat and round.

Step by step

  1. Work one stitch (for example, a single crochet) into the next stitch as normal.
  2. Insert your hook into the same stitch again.
  3. Work a second stitch of the same type into it.
  4. You have made one increase: two stitches now sit on top of one stitch from the round below.
  5. Continue with the pattern. In “(sc, inc) x6”, work 1 sc in the next stitch, then an increase in the following stitch, six times.
  6. Count at the end of the round. Six increases in a round of 12 stitches give you 18.

US vs UK name

In US patterns this is called Increase (inc). In UK patterns it's Increase (inc). The name is the same in both systems, which makes this one easy.

Where you'll use it

  • Flat circles and coasters
  • Amigurumi spheres and shapes
  • Shaping hats
  • Widening flat pieces

Common mistakes

  • Stacking increases in the same spot every round. This makes circles turn into hexagons. Some patterns stagger them, e.g. “sc, inc, (2 sc, inc) x5, sc”.
  • Losing count mid-round. Use a stitch marker at the start of the round and check the count in brackets at the end.
  • Working the increase into the wrong stitch. Both stitches must go into the same stitch.

Tip: Flat circles follow a simple rule: add the same number of increases each round as you had stitches in round 1. Six stitches in round 1 means six increases per round.
