How to Crochet: Slip Stitch (sl st)

The slip stitch is the shortest crochet stitch. It adds almost no height, so it's mainly used for joining and moving rather than building fabric.

You will use it to join the last stitch of a round to the first, to move your yarn across a few stitches without adding height, to attach pieces, and to make neat edges.

Abbreviation
sl st
US name
Slip stitch (sl st)
UK name
Slip stitch (ss)
Height
Almost none
Level
Beginner

Step by step

  1. Insert your hook into the stitch indicated by the pattern, going under both top loops.
  2. Yarn over.
  3. Pull the yarn through the stitch and straight through the loop on your hook in one movement.
  4. You now have one loop on your hook and one slip stitch made.
  5. To join a round, insert the hook into the first stitch of the round and work a slip stitch there.

US vs UK name

In US patterns this is called Slip stitch (sl st). In UK patterns it's Slip stitch (ss). The stitch is identical; only the name changes. Not sure which system your pattern uses? See our US vs UK crochet terms guide.

Where you'll use it

  • Joining rounds
  • Moving across stitches without adding height
  • Joining pieces together
  • Surface decoration and neat edges

Common mistakes

  • Working slip stitches too tightly. They tighten easily; keep a relaxed hand so you can work into them later.
  • Counting the joining slip stitch as a stitch. In most patterns the join doesn't count. Check the stitch count.
  • Joining into the wrong stitch. Mark the first stitch of each round so the join goes into the right place.

Tip: Amigurumi is often worked in a continuous spiral with no slip stitch joins at all. If your pattern doesn't say “join”, don't add one.
